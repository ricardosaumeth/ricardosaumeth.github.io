The Only Parts of Real‑Time UI Development AI Still Can't Do
(And How Seniors Fill the Gap)

AI is incredible at generating boilerplate, scaffolding components, and even writing reducers.

But in real‑time engineering — trading dashboards, RFQ systems, crypto feeds — there are still critical areas where AI simply can't replace senior judgment.

Here are the gaps that still require a human who understands systems, not just syntax.

1 — AI Can't Decide Where to Spend Complexity

Real‑time UIs are all about trade‑offs:

  • Freshness vs stability
  • Throughput vs CPU
  • Accuracy vs jitter

AI can generate code, but it can't decide which part of the system deserves complexity and which part must stay brutally simple.

That's architecture — not autocomplete.

2 — AI Can't Handle Messy, Imperfect Data

Real‑time data arrives:

  • Out of order
  • Duplicated
  • Missing fields
  • Faster than the UI can process

AI assumes the data is clean.
Senior engineers know it never is.

Only a human can design:

  • Schema validation
  • Backpressure
  • Domain‑aware reducers
  • Safe fallbacks when the feed lies

AI can write the code, but it can't define the truth model.

3 — AI Can't Predict How Real Users Will Lose Trust

Real‑time UIs fail when users stop believing what they see.

A senior engineer knows:

  • When a flicker is acceptable
  • When a 100ms delay is safer
  • When to batch updates
  • When to freeze the UI to avoid lying

AI can optimize for speed.
Only a human can optimize for trust.

The Gap Isn't Code — It's Judgment

AI accelerates the work.
Senior engineers decide what work actually matters.

Real‑time systems don't break because of missing code.
They break because of missing decisions.

And that's still human territory.
